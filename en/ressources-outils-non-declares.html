What to Do If Your Company Is Already Using Unregistered AI Tools

It's a more common situation than you might think: teams are already using AI every day, without leadership having a full picture. Here's how to regain control.

Business leaders often ask: "Are my teams already using AI?" The honest answer, in the vast majority of companies, is yes, even without a formal approval process. AI-assisted writing, translation, data analysis, image generation: these uses take hold on their own, one workstation at a time.

Why this matters

Since August 2, 2026, a new set of obligations under the European AI Act has come into force, with penalties that can reach €35 million or 7% of global revenue. An unrecorded use isn't necessarily risky in itself; but the lack of an overall picture makes it impossible to know.

The wrong answer: ban everything

Abruptly banning AI use rarely gets teams to stop: it pushes them to keep going quietly, without ever mentioning it to leadership again. The situation then gets worse: the usage persists, but becomes invisible.

The right answer: take stock before deciding

The first step isn't to judge, but to know. An honest inventory of the tools actually in use (including those never formally approved) makes it possible to distinguish low-risk uses from those that deserve to be regulated or stopped.

  • Which tools are used, by whom, and for what.
  • What data flows through them, particularly customer data or sensitive information.
  • The level of risk of each use with respect to the regulation.

Then, a framework rather than a ban

Once the situation is known, most companies don't need to ban AI: they need a clear framework for what is allowed, and an up-to-date register in case of an audit. That is precisely the purpose of a compliance diagnostic.

Official sources

  • Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of 13 June 2024 (AI Act) : official text published in the Official Journal of the European Union, on EUR-Lex.
  • European Commission, “AI Act” : application timeline and risk levels.

Timeline update: according to the European Commission, the digital omnibus on AI, in force since 27 July 2026, postpones to 2 December 2027 the obligations for high-risk systems listed in Annex III of the regulation, and to 2 August 2028 those for systems embedded in regulated products (Annex I). Sources checked on 11 October 2026. This page is for information only and is not legal advice.

A common starting point

A company that discovers several unregistered tools then has a natural conversation about what needs to be put in place: without overdramatizing, and without shutting everything down.
